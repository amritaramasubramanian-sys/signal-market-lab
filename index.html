INTERPRETATION, NOT A TRADE SIGNAL

A probability estimate is conditional on its assumptions. Compare the model with the market, then ask why they disagree. A gap may reflect stale information, different assumptions, model error, or compensation for risk.

A model-market gap is a research question, not proof of mispricing. Transaction costs, execution risk, and the quality of the underlying evidence can overwhelm an apparent edge.

WHY RELIABILITY MATTERS

The same headline should not move every analyst's estimate by the same amount. Evidence quality depends on source credibility, independence, sample size, timeliness, and whether the information was already reflected in prices.

With moderate reliability, the evidence shifts the prior but does not replace it.

CALIBRATION IS NOT CONFIDENCE

A forecast is calibrated if events assigned probability p occur about p percent of the time over many comparable forecasts. One correct prediction does not prove calibration. A small sample cannot establish skill.

Brier score = (forecast probability − outcome)²
Track forecasts consistently, preserve the original probability, and resolve only when the outcome is clear. Avoid editing past estimates after learning the result.

MODEL SPECIFICATION

The dashboard separates three concepts that are often conflated: an analyst's probability estimate, a market-implied price, and the cost of transacting at that price.

1 · EVIDENCE UPDATE

logit(posterior) = logit(prior) + reliability × signal

The signal is a user-controlled log-likelihood contribution. Reliability scales the contribution toward zero. This is a pedagogical parameterization, not a fitted model.

2 · UNCERTAINTY

Approx. interval = posterior ± 1.645 × σ

A simple symmetric band is shown for readability. Probabilities are bounded between 0% and 100%; this approximation is not a formal Bayesian credible interval.
